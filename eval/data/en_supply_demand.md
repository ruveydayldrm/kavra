# Principles of Microeconomics — Lecture 3: Supply, Demand and Market Equilibrium

## 1. Demand

Demand describes the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to purchase at each price. The law of demand states that, other things equal, the quantity demanded of a good falls when its price rises. A demand curve therefore slopes downward.

A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the demand curve, which economists call a change in quantity demanded. A change in any other determinant shifts the whole curve and is called a change in demand. Determinants that shift demand include consumer income, the prices of related goods, tastes, expectations and the number of buyers.

For a normal good, an increase in income increases demand. For an inferior good, an increase in income decreases demand; bus travel is a common example. Two goods are substitutes when a rise in the price of one increases the demand for the other, such as coffee and tea. Two goods are complements when a rise in the price of one decreases the demand for the other, such as cars and gasoline.

## 2. Supply

Supply describes the quantity that sellers are willing and able to sell at each price. The law of supply states that, other things equal, the quantity supplied rises when the price rises, so the supply curve slopes upward. Determinants that shift supply include input prices, technology, expectations and the number of sellers. A fall in the price of an input lowers production costs and shifts the supply curve to the right.

## 3. Equilibrium

Market equilibrium occurs at the price where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. If the price is above equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded and a surplus arises, which pushes the price down. If the price is below equilibrium, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied and a shortage arises, which pushes the price up.

When demand increases while supply is unchanged, both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity rise. When supply increases while demand is unchanged, the equilibrium price falls and the equilibrium quantity rises. If supply and demand both increase, the equilibrium quantity rises but the effect on price is ambiguous.

## 4. Elasticity

The price elasticity of demand measures how much the quantity demanded responds to a change in price. It is computed as the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price. Demand is elastic when this value is greater than one and inelastic when it is less than one.

Demand tends to be more elastic for goods with close substitutes, for luxuries rather than necessities, for narrowly defined markets and over longer time horizons. When demand is inelastic, a price increase raises total revenue. When demand is elastic, a price increase lowers total revenue.

## 5. Price controls

A price ceiling is a legal maximum price. A binding price ceiling is set below the equilibrium price and causes a shortage; rent control is a classic example. A price floor is a legal minimum price. A binding price floor is set above the equilibrium price and causes a surplus; the minimum wage can create a surplus of labour, which is unemployment.
